# Is the PBVI schedule more efficient than fixed 10-year / 5-year screening?

CMOST-in-the-loop, ages 40–80, 3 independent seeds × 200,000 individuals per
arm (600,000 total), common random numbers across arms.
Generated by `tests/surv_fair_compare.py` + `tests/surv_fair_report.py`;
raw numbers in `results/survfair/` (`report.txt`, `summary.csv`).

---

## 1. How post-diagnosis follow-up was handled, and why

CMOST has two follow-up streams, each behind its own flag:

| flag | fires | keyed on |
|---|---|---|
| `Polyp_Surveillance` | 3y after an advanced adenoma, 5y after any adenoma, then ≥5y | `Last_Polyp`, `Last_AdvPolyp` |
| `Cancer_Surveillance` | 1y, 4y, then ≥5y after resection | `Last_Cancer` |

**Neither flag touches survival after diagnosis.** `Detected_MortTime` is drawn
from stage at diagnosis alone, identically in every arm. The flags only add
colonoscopies (`Follow_Up_Colonoscopy`, detection mode 3). So the fairness
question is not about prognosis — it is about which colonoscopies land on the
budget axis that λ prices.

**Both streams are triggered by a finding, so both are endogenous to screening
intensity.** q5y removes more adenomas than q10y and earns more post-polypectomy
follow-up for it; it also diagnoses more cancers and earns more post-resection
follow-up. Turning cancer surveillance off while leaving polyp surveillance on
removes the *smaller* confound and keeps the larger one — measured here,
polyp surveillance is roughly 6× the volume (q10y follow-up 0.738
colonoscopies/person, of which a no-screening cohort — where only cancer
surveillance can fire — generates 0.119). It also matches no practice pattern:
no guideline surveils adenomas but not resections. The result would be a budget
axis that is neither the clean decision problem nor clinical reality.

The two coherent choices, run here as primary and sensitivity:

- **Primary — both OFF.** Every colonoscopy is one a decision rule chose, so the
  budget axis is exactly "colonoscopies spent", and the POMDP's own world model
  (which has no surveillance concept) matches the simulator it is evaluated in.
  Isolates the scheduling rule.
- **Sensitivity — both ON, identically in every arm.** Compare on *total* volume
  (screening + follow-up). This is guideline practice, and it is the conservative
  test: the policy arm is now mis-specified relative to its training world, a
  handicap the fixed arms do not carry.

### Keeping the arms symmetric when surveillance is on

CMOST fires surveillance immediately *before* the policy decision in the same
`q==1` slot. Without coordination the policy arm is billed two colonoscopies in
a year where a coordinated arm is billed one — an accounting artifact with no
clinical content. A new engine callback, `policy_hook.note_colonoscopy(z, y,
kind, s_true)`, reports every scope the hook did not order:

- **fixed arms** — `min_gap` = the schedule's own interval, i.e. a surveillance
  scope substitutes for one screening round (the guideline convention that
  follow-up resets the screening clock).
- **policy arm** — `screen_min_gap=1` (never two scopes in one year; its clock is
  its belief, not an interval) plus `react_to_external_colo`, which swaps that
  year's belief transition from WAIT to the SCREEN one the follow-up scope
  actually produced. The *policy* is still trained surveillance-free.

## 2. Primary analysis — surveillance OFF

CRC deaths per 100,000, mean ± SEM across 3 seeds:

| arm | colonoscopies/person | CRC deaths/100k | high:low colo ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| no screening | 0.050 | 1912.8 ± 35.9 | 4.80 |
| **q10y** | **2.607** | **1025.5 ± 12.0** | **1.01** |
| **q5y** | **5.020** | **795.3 ± 10.6** | **1.00** |
| policy λ=+0.010 | 1.717 | 1033.5 ± 15.9 | 5.89 |
| policy λ=+0.005 | 1.850 | 956.5 ± 12.8 | 6.64 |
| policy λ=+0.002 | 2.196 | 900.3 ± 8.6 | 5.79 |
| policy λ=0.000 | 2.608 | 867.5 ± 15.8 | 4.76 |
| policy λ=−0.002 | 2.691 | 834.8 ± 10.5 | 4.51 |
| policy λ=−0.004 | 3.255 | 817.5 ± 10.8 | 3.96 |
| policy λ=−0.006 | 4.445 | 735.8 ± 5.4 | 2.88 |
| policy λ=−0.008 | 5.204 | 690.0 ± 3.8 | 2.58 |

**The policy dominates both comparators** — strictly better on *both* axes at
once, no interpolation and no trade-off weight required:

| | vs q10y (λ=+0.002) | vs q5y (λ=−0.006) |
|---|---|---|
| colonoscopies | 2.196 vs 2.607 (**−15.8%**) | 4.445 vs 5.020 (**−11.5%**) |
| CRC deaths/100k | 900.3 vs 1025.5 (**−12.2%**) | 735.8 vs 795.3 (**−7.5%**) |
| mortality difference | 125.2 ± 14.8 (**8.5 SE**) | 59.5 ± 11.9 (**5.0 SE**) |
| discounted cost/person | $1,950 vs $2,348 (**−17%**) | $3,442 vs $3,699 (**−7%**) |

Read the other two ways:

- **matched budget** — at q10y's 2.607 colonoscopies the policy reaches 867.5
  deaths/100k vs 1025.5 (**−15.4%**); at q5y's 5.020 it reaches 701.1 vs 795.3
  (**−11.8%**).
- **matched effect** — to reach q10y's mortality the policy needs 1.731
  colonoscopies vs 2.607 (**−33.6%**); to reach q5y's, 3.578 vs 5.020
  (**−28.7%**).

## 3. Sensitivity analysis — surveillance ON

| arm | colonoscopies/person | (screening / follow-up) | CRC deaths/100k |
|---|---|---|---|
| no screening | 0.167 | 0.000 / 0.119 | 1819.5 ± 2.6 |
| **q10y** | **3.125** | 2.368 / 0.738 | **823.5 ± 7.6** |
| **q5y** | **5.333** | 4.417 / 0.904 | **659.8 ± 6.7** |
| policy λ=+0.002 | 2.680 | 1.927 / 0.735 | 816.8 ± 6.1 |
| policy λ=−0.001 | 3.103 | 2.332 / 0.755 | 764.7 ± 8.4 |
| policy λ=−0.006 | 4.858 | 4.136 / 0.709 | 679.7 ± 14.8 |
| policy λ=−0.008 | 5.585 | 4.857 / 0.716 | 604.3 ± 6.6 |

- **vs q10y — the conclusion holds.** λ=+0.002 delivers statistically
  indistinguishable mortality (816.8 vs 823.5, difference 6.7 ± 9.7 — 0.7 SE)
  with **14.2% fewer colonoscopies**. λ=−0.001 instead spends the same volume
  (3.103 vs 3.125) for **58.8 ± 11.3 fewer deaths/100k (5.2 SE, −7.1%)**.
  Three λ values dominate q10y outright.
- **vs q5y — the advantage shrinks to a small edge, and dominance is lost.**
  At matched budget the policy reaches 630.5 vs 659.8 (−4.5%); at matched
  effect it needs 5.050 vs 5.333 colonoscopies (−5.3%). No λ beats q5y on both
  axes. The closest point, λ=−0.008, buys 55.5 fewer deaths/100k for 4.7% more
  volume — a favourable trade, but a trade.

### Why surveillance compresses the gap

Because **CMOST's surveillance is itself a risk-adaptive mechanism.** It routes
extra colonoscopies to people who have had findings — which is a noisy version
of exactly what the PBVI policy does from a risk label. Switching it on hands
the fixed schedules a large slice of the policy's own mechanism for free:

| arm | high:low colonoscopy ratio | high-risk CRC deaths/100k |
|---|---|---|
| q10y, surveillance off | 1.01 | 2308.4 |
| q10y, surveillance on | **1.52** | **1338.1** |
| q5y, surveillance off | 1.00 | 1588.1 |
| q5y, surveillance on | **1.17** | **1054.1** |

Post-polypectomy follow-up is extremely efficient in its own right — for q10y it
adds 0.518 colonoscopies/person and averts 202 deaths/100k, about 390 per
colonoscopy-unit, better than q10y's own screening yield of 340. That is the
substantive reason the half-on configuration must be avoided: it is not a minor
bookkeeping detail, it is a second adaptive policy running underneath the one
being measured.

## 4. Mechanism: allocation, not timing

The policy's high:low colonoscopy ratio runs 2.6–6.6 against 1.00–1.01 for the
uniform schedules (figure `surv_fair_risk_targeting.png`, panel a). High-risk
CRC mortality falls from 2308 (q10y) and 1588 (q5y) to 967–1195 across the
λ range that beats the fixed schedules overall (−0.008 to +0.002), while
low-risk mortality stays comparable at a fraction of the volume.

**But the gain is reallocation across strata, not better timing within one.**
Panel (b) plots each stratum's mortality against that stratum's own colonoscopy
volume, and the fixed schedules sit essentially *on* the policy's per-stratum
curve:

- low risk, primary: q10y (2.602 → 702.2) vs the policy curve interpolated at
  2.602 → ≈706. Indistinguishable.
- high risk, primary: q5y (5.022 → 1588.1) vs the policy at 5.098 → 1677.5.
  If anything the uniform 5-yearly schedule is *slightly better* at matched
  within-stratum volume.

So the belief-driven adaptivity is not, on this evidence, beating a fixed
interval at a given intensity. What it is doing — and what the fixed schedules
structurally cannot do — is choose *different intensities for different people*.
That is the claim the numbers support.

## 5. What these numbers do not show

1. **The risk label is an oracle.** high_risk = top 20% of CMOST's own
   `individual_risk`, disclosed to the agent at t=0. A deployable risk score is
   noisier, so every advantage here is an **upper bound**. (The repo's Jeon-2018
   composite bucket-maps back onto the same `individual_risk` pool, so it
   reproduces nearly the same partition — that is a property of the construction,
   not independent validation.)
2. **Life-years cannot separate the arms.** Discounted LY differences between
   screening arms are 0.01–0.02 against a between-seed SEM of 0.004–0.010.
   Screening vs no screening is detectable (+0.054 for the best policy arm,
   ~10 SE); arm vs arm is not.
   CRC is a small share of all-cause mortality at these ages, so CRC mortality
   is the only endpoint with enough signal — and ICERs, whose denominator is
   that LY difference, are not interpretable at this sample size and are
   deliberately not reported.
3. **The policy is trained surveillance-free in both analyses.** The sensitivity
   arm only lets it *observe* follow-up scopes, not plan around them. A policy
   trained in a surveillance-aware world would presumably do better than the
   sensitivity numbers show; that run has not been done.
4. **100% adherence everywhere**, and the fixed comparators screen at exact
   ages, matching the convention used elsewhere in this repo rather than any
   real programme's uptake.
5. **One asymmetry left in the primary analysis.** With surveillance off, the
   policy still declines to scope in a year when a symptom-triggered colonoscopy
   already happened, while the fixed arms have no such rule. Symptom scopes run
   0.013–0.024 per person, so the clash probability is on the order of 10⁻³ —
   below the resolution of everything reported above, but it is not exactly zero.

---

## 6. Bottom line

With post-diagnosis follow-up switched off in every arm — the comparison that
isolates the scheduling rule — the PBVI policy **strictly dominates both fixed
schedules**: 16% fewer colonoscopies *and* 12% fewer CRC deaths than q10y, 12%
fewer colonoscopies *and* 8% fewer CRC deaths than q5y. Equivalently, it reaches
either schedule's mortality with about a third fewer colonoscopies.

With guideline follow-up switched on in every arm, the advantage over q10y
survives (same mortality for 14% fewer colonoscopies, or 7% lower mortality at
the same volume) but the advantage over q5y narrows to roughly 5%, and no λ
dominates q5y outright. The reason is interpretable rather than awkward:
CMOST's finding-triggered surveillance is itself risk-adaptive, so turning it on
gives the uniform schedules part of the mechanism the PBVI policy was built to
supply.
